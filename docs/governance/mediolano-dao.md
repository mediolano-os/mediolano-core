# Mediolano DAO

*Who stewards the commons, how it's funded, and how anyone — human or AI — takes part. Plain idea
first, then the substance, then links for depth.*

---

The Mediolano DAO is the steward of a public good. Its job is to keep the protocol neutral, open,
and durable over time — not to own it, and not to profit from it. The primitives belong to
everyone; the DAO tends them on everyone's behalf.

## What the DAO is committed to

- **Sustainability** — the continuous improvement and long-term survival of the protocol and its
  services.
- **Public benefit** — promoting open-source technology that serves the broader public and the
  creator economy.
- **Censorship-resistant ownership** — true ownership of data on infrastructure that cannot be
  censored.
- **Security & transparency** — robust security and transparent, verifiable control mechanisms for
  all participants.
- **Privacy** — privacy upheld as a fundamental human right in the digital age.

## How it's funded

**The protocol primitives are zero-fee; the DAO funds itself off-protocol.**

The tokenization and protection primitives take nothing — that is a hard invariant. The DAO
sustains its stewardship **off-protocol**: through optional premium services and app-layer
offerings, grants, and donations. Any surplus is recycled into public goods — protocol development,
open-source tooling, and support for the wider commons. The DAO never meters or taxes the
primitives to fund itself.

## Membership and voting

**Membership is open and held through the MIP token; every token is a vote.**

- **Who can join** — individuals, organizations, and DAOs, as well as **AI agents and autonomous
  intelligence** with verifiable credentials. Participation is open to any form of intelligence
  that supports the DAO's purpose.
- **Voting** — governance happens on **Snapshot**; **one MIP token equals one unit of voting
  power**; a simple majority decides unless a higher bar is specified.
- **Delegation** — voting power can be delegated; a minimum holding may apply to *submit* a
  proposal.

The governing details live in the [Constitution](constitution.md) and
[Governance Charter](governance-charter.md) (both currently drafts pending ratification).

## Working groups and SubDAOs

To manage operations, the DAO may form specialized working groups and SubDAOs — for areas such as
protocol development, community, and other verticals — each accountable to the DAO.

## Stewardship without extraction

The DAO governs **neutrality and resilience**, never gates and never rents: decisions stay opt-in
and never become barriers to permissionless use, the commons is kept open and uncapturable, and
stewardship itself decentralizes over time.
