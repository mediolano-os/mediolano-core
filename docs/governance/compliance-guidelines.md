# Compliance Guidelines

> **This is general information, not legal advice.** It describes Mediolano's compliance posture at
> a high level. It makes no claims about the legal or regulatory status of any token, asset, or
> activity in any jurisdiction. Consult qualified counsel for your situation.

---

Mediolano is neutral public infrastructure. Its compliance posture follows from what it is — a
non-custodial protocol that records and proves, rather than a service that holds assets or
adjudicates rights.

## Non-custodial and neutral

Mediolano never holds, controls, or restricts user assets, and it authorizes nothing — the
contracts are permissionless and users act directly through them. There is no intermediary taking
custody and no operator who can move or freeze what belongs to a user.

→ Go deeper: [Protocol & Applications](../architecture/03-protocol-and-apps.md).

## Authorship and intellectual property

Mediolano produces durable, independently verifiable records of authorship, designed to align with
the Berne Convention. Importantly, the protocol **records; it does not adjudicate**. It creates
evidence that strengthens a creator's position wherever rights are recognized and enforced — it does
not grant, decide, or enforce copyright on anyone's behalf.

→ Go deeper: [Licensing & Authorship](../architecture/05-licensing-and-authorship.md).

## On securities and financial regulation

This documentation makes **no** assertion that any token, asset, or activity associated with
Mediolano is — or is not — a security, financial instrument, or regulated product in any
jurisdiction. Rules vary widely by country and change over time. Participants are responsible for
understanding and meeting the requirements that apply to them, and should seek qualified legal
advice. Specific regulatory questions are out of scope for this document and flagged for legal
review.

## Data and privacy

Mediolano treats privacy as a right and favors data minimization — proving claims without revealing
the data behind them, and not accreting personal data the protocol does not need. A dedicated
privacy policy accompanies the applications; details of data handling belong there (a separate
document).
